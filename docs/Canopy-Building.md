# Building a canopy



This example is five blocks wide and five blocks deep. Each letter in the diagram is a separate block position. Count the first block space above the platform as level 0.

1. Place two matching canopy columns at the left and right ends of the middle row. Stack each three sections high (levels 0, 1 and 2). Holding the column item and right-clicking extends it upward.
2. At level 3, connect the columns with five matching canopy beams. Wrench them so they run across the five-block width. For a traditional canopy, optionally replace the beam directly above each column with a Traditional Canopy Support Bracket.
3. Place a five-by-five roof at level 4, directly above the support/trim layer. Roof panels sit at the bottom of their cells. The two glazed rows in the diagram are optional; solid panels work there too.
4. Place five fascia sections along the front at level 3, and five along the back at level 3. Face the front fascia outward toward the track, and rotate the back row to face outward in the opposite direction. Fascia belongs in the row below the roof, where it hangs down from the edge.
5. Put Under-canopy Strip Lights in the two remaining level-3 rows, between the fascia and centre beam. Each light uses its own cell. Use temporary blocks or scaffolding to position thin pieces, then remove the temporary blocks.
6. Repeat the middle sections to extend the canopy. Retain clear space for benches, signs and walking routes.

## Pieces for the illustrated example

| Piece | Quantity |
|---|---:|
| Modern or Traditional Canopy Column | 6 |
| Matching Canopy Beam | 5 |
| Matching Canopy Roof Panel | 15 |
| Matching Canopy Glazed Roof | 10 |
| Matching Fascia / Traditional Scalloped Fascia | 10 |
| Under-canopy Strip Light | 10 |

For the bracket option, use three beams and two Traditional Canopy Support Brackets instead of five beams. A solid roof uses 25 roof panels and no glazed panels.

Normal Create wrench clicks rotate individual parts. Sneak-wrench picks up the selected part. Thin decorative pieces still occupy their own whole block position, so fascia, beams and lights cannot share a cell.

## Fascia corner update
Modern and traditional fascia now have inside and outside corners. Put corner pieces in the fascia layer below the roof, replacing straight trim at each turn. Wrench-rotate to align both legs, then continue straight trim down each edge. The old open-sided example can be extended into a full perimeter: relocate any light or beam that occupies a new trim cell. Complete recipes are in STATION_BUILDING.md.

## Traditional roof finishes
The solid traditional roof uses felt above and white painted planks below, with pale edge trim. Rotate the roof with the Create wrench to align the underside planks. Glazed roof sections remain transparent.
